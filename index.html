I am a researcher at the University of Zurich and in the Multilinguality and Language Technology department at DFKI, on the Saarland Informatics Campus, where I work with Josef van Genabith and Cristina España-Bonet.

My research is on sign language translation — mapping continuous signing video into spoken-language text. Most systems in this area lean on glosses: manual annotations that are expensive to produce and exist for only a handful of languages. My work asks how far we can get without them. That means supervising translation with sentence embeddings in place of glosses, extending that supervision to the multilingual, language-agnostic setting, and learning spatio-temporal representations end to end.

More recently I have been asking whether these systems are actually watching the video. Fluent output is easy to mistake for correct output, so I study visual signals that separate grounded translation from hallucination, what our evaluation metrics really measure, and how well the corpora the field has standardised on hold up under audit.

I work on multimodal NLP more broadly too — video-grounded fact verification, vision-language pipelines for data curation, and multilingual model behaviour.

Earlier, I contributed to AVASAG, a German Sign Language translation system for public services built around a 3D signing avatar.

selected publications

  1. ICLR
    Grounding or Guessing? Visual Signals for Detecting Hallucinations in Sign Language Translation
    Yasser Hamidullah, Koel Dutta Chowdhury, Yusser Al Ghussin, and 4 more authors
    In The Fourteenth International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR), 2026
    Abs Bib HTML Code

    Sign language translation systems can produce fluent output that is not grounded in the signing video. We study visual signals that reveal when a model is grounding its translation in the video versus guessing from language priors, and use them to detect hallucinations.

  2. LREC
    A Critical Study of Automatic Evaluation in Sign Language Translation
    Shakib Yazdani, Yasser Hamidullah, Cristina España-Bonet, and 2 more authors
    In Proceedings of the Fifteenth Language Resources and Evaluation Conference (LREC), May 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    Automatic evaluation metrics are crucial for advancing sign language translation (SLT). This work examines how faithfully current metrics reflect translation quality in the SLT setting.

  3. WMT
    SONAR-SLT: Multilingual Sign Language Translation via Language-Agnostic Sentence Embedding Supervision
    Yasser Hamidullah, Shakib Yazdani, Cennet Oguz, and 2 more authors
    In Proceedings of the Tenth Conference on Machine Translation (WMT), Nov 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    Extends sentence-embedding supervision for sign language translation to the multilingual, language-agnostic setting using SONAR embeddings, removing the dependency on glosses while supporting translation into many spoken languages.

  4. ACL
    Sign Language Translation with Sentence Embedding Supervision
    Yasser Hamidullah, Josef Genabith, and Cristina España-Bonet
    In Proceedings of the 62nd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 2: Short Papers), Aug 2024
    Abs DOI Bib Code

    State-of-the-art sign language translation systems rely on gloss annotations, which are costly and scarce. We instead supervise training with sentence embeddings of the target sentences, which take the role of glosses but require no manual annotation and are learned from raw textual data.

  5. WMT
    Spatio-temporal Sign Language Representation and Translation
    Yasser Hamidullah, Josef Genabith, and Cristina España-Bonet
    In Proceedings of the Seventh Conference on Machine Translation (WMT), Dec 2022
    Abs arXiv Bib

    This paper describes the DFKI-MLT submission to the WMT-SLT 2022 sign language translation task from Swiss German Sign Language (video) into German (text). Instead of relying on features that ignore temporal structure, we present a system that learns spatio-temporal feature representations and translation in a single model, resulting in a real end-to-end architecture expected to better generalize to new data sets.
